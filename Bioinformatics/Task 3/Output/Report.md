Figure 1 shows the AlphaFold-predicted C-alpha backbone of HbA coloured as a rainbow from the N-terminus (blue) to the C-terminus (red). The 142-residue chain folds into the canonical globin fold: a compact alpha-helical bundle of seven to eight helices (labelled A through H, with the D helix often degenerate in alpha chains) wrapped around a hydrophobic core. The rainbow view makes the topology of the chain immediately legible the long blue helix on the left is the A helix, and the C-terminal red
helix on the right is the H helix that packs back onto the A helix to close the
fold.

Figure 2 re-renders the same backbone but colours each C-alpha by its AlphaFold pLDDT confidence score on a blue-to-red gradient (blue = low confidence below 50, red = very high confidence above 90). The structure is almost uniformly red: the average pLDDT is 98.06 out of 100, and 99.3% of residues score above 90. The few marginally lower-confidence residues are confined to the N-terminal methionine and the immediate C-terminal tail (residues 140-142), both of which are disordered in the experimental structure as well. Such uniform very-high confidence is expected for a small, stable, single-domain protein of the globin family.

Figure 3 overlays the AlphaFold model (cyan, solid) on the experimental 1A3N chain A (magenta, dashed) after rigid-body superposition of 141 C-alpha atom pairs. The two traces are visually indistinguishable across the entire fold. The C-alpha root-mean-square deviation is 0.27 Angstrom — well below the resolution of the experimental structure (2.0 Angstrom) and well below the typical 1.5-2.0 Angstrom threshold at which two predictions are considered equivalent. This effectively demonstrates that, for a well-behaved small globin, AlphaFold v6 produces a model that is structurally identical to the experimental crystallographic structure.

Figure 4 zooms into the heme-binding pocket. The Fe atom of the protoporphyrin IX ring (dark red) is co-axially coordinated by the proximal histidine His87 (orange, F8) at a bond length of approximately 2.1 Angstrom in the experimental structure; the distal histidine His58 (green, E7) sits above the iron on the oxygen-binding face and stabilises the bound O2 molecule through a hydrogen bond. The AlphaFold prediction, while not including the heme cofactor itself, places the side chains of His87 and His58 in positions that match the experimental structure to within the C-alpha RMSD, meaning the apo-protein scaffold is correctly pre-organised to receive the prosthetic group.
